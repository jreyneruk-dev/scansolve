# Vertical brief — Gyms & leisure

Slug: `gyms` · Wave 1 (deep) · Last researched 2026-07-06

This brief is the evidence base for the `/for/gyms` page and the reference example
for the `scansolve-vertical-page` skill. Everything on the page should trace to a
line here. Claims marked with a source are usable in copy; unsourced ones are
framing, not facts.

## Buyer
- Role: independent gym owner / operator, or the duty / general manager of a single
  site or a small group (2–5 sites). Owner-operated = fast yes, no procurement.
- What they're judged on: member retention and the member experience. Broken kit and
  a scruffy changing room show up as cancellations and one-star reviews.

## The sharp angle (what this page leads on)
Not "QR reporting exists" — competitors already sell that to gyms. The wedge is
**price and friction**: unlimited labels free forever and one flat £15/mo for the whole
team, against incumbents that bill per user per month. A gym doesn't need a $16–20 per-head
CMMS to log a broken treadmill.

## Pain, in the operator's own words
- Members don't report faults — they stop coming and post about it. A competitor
  (Jotform) sells QR reporting explicitly as fixing broken kit "before becoming negative
  reviews," which confirms the felt problem. [src: Jotform]
- Staff know a machine's been down for a week but it never got logged, so it never got
  booked for repair. (Framing from ICP; matches the "reactive, invoice-driven" pattern.)
- Equipment availability is a named driver of churn — members quit gyms where kit is
  broken or they wait for it. [src: PerfectGym / retention research]

## Concrete faults to name (specific beats "issues")
Treadmill out of order · shower running cold · locker won't lock · cable/pulley frayed ·
air con not working · toilet blocked · free weights missing/racked wrong · door fob failing ·
soap/paper dispenser empty · matting torn (trip hazard).

## Proof stats (sourced — usable in copy)
- Gyms lose up to ~50% of members a year; the average membership lasts ~7–8 months, and
  retention-focused clubs push that past 12. [src: smarthealthclubs / Resawod retention data]
- Members who visit ≥2×/week are ~50% less likely to cancel (IHRSA) — i.e. the in-club
  experience, including working kit, is what keeps them. [src: IHRSA via retention research]
- Use as the page's proof-stat band: **"Gyms lose up to half their members every year —
  and a broken machine a member never reports is a renewal you never see."** (Stat sourced;
  the causal link is framed as opinion, not claimed as a measured figure.)

## Competitor teardown → the gap we exploit
- MaintainX: Essentials **$16/user/mo**, Premium **$49/user/mo**; sells to fitness. [src: MaintainX pricing / Capterra]
- UpKeep: from **$20/user/mo**, no permanent free plan; has a dedicated gym-equipment page. [src: UpKeep]
- Niche QR-for-gyms tools already exist: FixTag (QR stickers, no app, live dashboard),
  Oxmaint (QR work orders on a CMMS), Xenia, TARGPatrol. So "QR + no app" alone is not a
  differentiator.
- **The gap:** they price per user, so a 10-person gym team is a per-head bill that grows.
  ScanSolve is flat £15/mo for owner + 20, and unlimited labels are free forever. Lead with
  the flat price and the free-unlimited-labels moat, not the QR mechanic.
- Competitor-contrast line for the page: **"MaintainX and UpKeep bill $16–20 per user, per
  month. ScanSolve is one flat price for your whole team — and putting a code on every
  machine costs nothing."**
